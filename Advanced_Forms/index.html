2nd Dan Black Belt & Higher Advanced Forms Guide

ITF & Kukkiwon Advanced Black Belt Tul & Poomsae Curriculum (Forms 12+)

PO-EUN (포은)
2nd Dan Black Belt
36 Movements | Diagram: Single Straight Horizontal Line (-)
Historical Meaning: Pseudonym of Chong Mong-Chu (1400 AD), a loyal subject and poet whose poem "I would not serve another master though I were crucified a hundred times" is known to every Korean. The diagram represents his unyielding, single-minded loyalty to his king and country.
GE-BAEK (계백)
2nd Dan Black Belt
44 Movements | Diagram: Capital I (Modified)
Historical Meaning: Named after General Ge-Baek, a great general in the Paekje Dynasty (660 AD). The diagram represents his strict and severe military discipline in defending his homeland.
EUI-AM (의암)
2nd Dan Black Belt
45 Movements | Diagram: Capital I
Historical Meaning: Pseudonym of Son Byong Hi, leader of the Korean independence movement on March 1, 1919. The 45 movements refer to his age when he changed the name of Dong Hak (Eastern Learning) to Chondo Kyo (Heavenly Way Religion) in 1905.
CHOONG-JANG (충장)
3rd Dan Black Belt
52 Movements | Diagram: Capital I
Historical Meaning: Pseudonym given to General Kim Duk Ryong who lived during the Yi Dynasty (14th century). Pattern ends with a left-hand attack to symbolize his tragic execution in prison at age 27 before reaching maturity.
JUCHE (주체)
3rd Dan Black Belt
45 Movements | Diagram: Mountain Symbol 🏔️
Historical Meaning: Philosophical idea that man is the master of everything and decides everything. Replaced Ko-Dang in the official ITF syllabus in the 1980s. Emphasizes flying kicks, slow control movements, and high technical precision.
SAM-IL (삼일)
3rd Dan Black Belt
33 Movements | Diagram: Roman Numeral I
Historical Meaning: Denotes the historic date of the March 1st Movement (3/1/1919) which sparked the Korean independence movement against Japanese occupation. The 33 movements represent the 33 Korean patriots who signed the Declaration of Independence.
YOO-SIN (유신)
3rd Dan Black Belt
68 Movements | Diagram: Capital I
Historical Meaning: Named after General Kim Yoo Sin, commanding general during the Silla Dynasty. The 68 movements refer to 668 AD, the year Korea was united. The ready stance signifies a sword drawn on the right side rather than the left side, symbolizing Yoo Sin's mistake of following his king's order to fight with foreign forces against his own nation.
CHOI-YONG (최영)
3rd Dan Black Belt
46 Movements | Diagram: Capital I
Historical Meaning: Named after General Choi Yong, premier and commander-in-chief of the armed forces during the 14th century Koryo Dynasty. Choi Yong was greatly respected for his loyalty, patriotism, and simplicity.
KEUMGANG (금강)
2nd Dan Black Belt (Kukkiwon)
27 Movements | Diagram: Mountain Symbol 山
Historical Meaning: Symbolizes 'hardness' and 'ponderance', derived from Mt. Keumgang (the majestic mountain) and Keumgang-seok (the diamond). Features Crane Stances (*Hakdari-Seogi*), Diamond Blocks (*Keumgang-Makgi*), and Santul Blocks.
TAEBAEK (태백)
3rd Dan Black Belt (Kukkiwon)
30 Movements | Diagram: Scholar Symbol 土 / 山
Historical Meaning: Named after Mt. Taebaek, the sacred mountain where Tangun, founder of the Korean nation, established the kingdom. Symbolizes light, holiness, and human welfare (*Hongik-Ingan*).
