1. Chang-Hon 1st Dan Context & 2nd Dan Black Belt Forms

Po-Eun (포은) & Ge-Baek (계백)

1st Dan • Chang-Hon Tul

In standard Chang-Hon Taekwon-Do, Kwang-Gae (39 moves), Po-Eun (36 moves, representing Chong Mong-Ju's unwavering loyalty on a single horizontal line —), and Ge-Baek (44 moves, representing General Gyebaek's military discipline on diagram I) form the complete triad of 1st Dan patterns.

Eui-Am (의암)

2nd Dan • 45 Movements

Named after Son Byong-Hi, leader of the Korean independence movement on March 1, 1919. The 45 movements refer to his age when he changed the name of Donghak to Cheondogyo in 1905.

Choong-Jang (충장)

2nd Dan • 52 Movements

Named after General Kim Deok-ryeong of the Joseon Dynasty. The pattern ends with a left-hand strike symbolizing his tragic death in prison in 1596 at age 27 after being falsely accused of treason.

Juche / Ko-Dang (주체 / 고당)

2nd Dan • 45 / 39 Movements

Ko-Dang was named after independence patriot Cho Man-sik. In the 1980s, ITF replaced Ko-Dang with Juche, reflecting the philosophical principle of self-reliance.

Keumgang Poomsae (금강 품새)

2nd Dan • Kukkiwon Yudanja Poomsae • 27 Movements

Named after Mt. Keumgang (Diamond Mountain) and the diamond symbol (*Vajra*), representing hardness, wisdom, and majestic stillness. The floor diagram traces the Chinese character for mountain (山) and features the signature Crane Stance (Hakdari-seogi).

2. 3rd Dan Black Belt Forms & School Weapons Form

Sam-Il (삼일)

3rd Dan • 33 Movements

Denotes the historic March 1, 1919 Independence Movement. The 33 movements commemorate the 33 Korean national patriots who signed the Declaration of Independence.

Yoo-Sin (유신)

3rd Dan • 68 Movements

Named after General Kim Yoo-Sin, commanding general during the Silla Dynasty who led the unification of the Three Kingdoms of Korea in 668 AD.

Choi-Yong (최영)

3rd Dan • 46 Movements

Named after General Choi Yong, premier and commander of the armed forces of the 14th century Goryeo Dynasty, renowned for his loyalty and patriotism.

Taebaek Poomsae (태백 품새)

3rd Dan • Kukkiwon Yudanja Poomsae • 26 Movements

Named after the "bright mountain" associated with the sacred Asadal tradition and the mythical founding of Korea by Dangun, with Mt. Baekdu as its representative place. The floor diagram traces the Korean character 工, symbolizing craftsmanship and connection between heaven and earth.

Peong-Hwa (평화)

1st Dan Black Belt (UMA Curriculum) • 66 Movements

School curriculum form signifying "Peace through Strength". Mastered alongside weapons forms (Bo staff, Sword, and Nunchaku).

3. Academic & Technical Reference Bibliography

Choi, Hong Hi (General) Encyclopedia of Taekwon-Do (15 Volumes, 1985; 1999 Condensed Edition) International Taekwon-Do Federation. ISBN: 978-1897307007. Definitive technical source for Chang-Hon Tul (Forms #1–#10), stance geometry, and prearranged sparring (Vol. 5).
Choi, Hong Hi (General) Taekwon-Do: The Art of Self-Defence (1965) Daeha Publication Company, Seoul. Historical source for original step-sparring, grab releases, and early curriculum foundations.
Kukkiwon (World Taekwondo Headquarters) Taekwondo Textbook & Technical Terminology Glossary Kukkiwon, Seoul, Republic of Korea. Official standard for Kukkiwon Poomsae (Koryo, Keumgang, Taebaek) and 4-pillar self-defense taxonomy.
World Health Organization (WHO) Standard Acupuncture Nomenclature & Point Locations (1991 / 2008) WHO Regional Office for the Western Pacific, Manila. ISBN: 978-9290613831. Standard international anatomical locations and alphanumeric codes for vital point acupuncture nomenclature.
Master Tom Malone Tae Kwon Do Personal Notes & Curriculum Syllabus Ultimate Martial Arts (UMA), Private Syllabus. Primary curriculum notes for belt-by-belt one-steps, kicking routines, grab releases, and breaking standards.
