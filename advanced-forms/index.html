1. 2nd Dan Black Belt Forms (ITF & Kukkiwon)

Pattern Name
Degree
Movements
Historical Significance & Theme
Eui-Am (의암)
2nd Dan
45 Moves
Named after Son Byong-Hi, leader of the March 1st 1919 Korean Independence Movement. The 45 movements refer to his age when he changed the Donghak religion to Cheondogyo.
Choong-Jang (충장)
2nd Dan
52 Moves
Named after General Kim Deok-ryeong (1567-1596 AD) of the Joseon Dynasty. The pattern ends with a left-handed attack to symbolize his tragic execution in prison.
Juche / Ko-Dang (주체 / 고당)
2nd Dan
45 / 39 Moves
Ko-Dang was named after Korean nationalist Cho Man-sik (1883-1950). In 1986, General Choi replaced Ko-Dang with Juche (45 moves). Traditional lineages retain Ko-Dang (39 moves).
Keumgang (금강)
2nd Dan WT
27 Moves
Official Kukkiwon 2nd Dan Poomsae representing the diamond and Mt. Geumgang, symbolizing unbreakable hardness and majestic spiritual clarity (Crane stance & Mountain blocks).

2. 3rd Dan Black Belt Forms (ITF & Kukkiwon)

Pattern Name
Degree
Movements
Historical Significance & Theme
Sam-Il (삼일)
3rd Dan
33 Moves
Commemorates the historic March 1st 1919 Korean Independence Movement. The 33 movements represent the 33 Korean patriots who signed the Declaration of Independence.
Yoo-Sin (유신)
3rd Dan
68 Moves
Named after General Kim Yoo-Sin, commanding general during the Silla Dynasty who united the Three Kingdoms of Korea in 668 AD. The 68 movements refer to the last two figures of 668 AD.
Choi-Yong (최영)
3rd Dan
46 Moves
Named after General Choi Yong, premier and commander-in-chief of the armed forces during the 14th century Goryeo Dynasty, famed for his loyalty and patriotism.
Taebaek (태백)
3rd Dan WT
26 Moves
Official Kukkiwon 3rd Dan Poomsae named after Mt. Taebaek, the sacred mountain where Dangun Wanggeom founded Gojoseon. Traces the Chinese character 工 (26 movements).
