Source & Scope: This page contextualizes advanced forms from General Choi Hong Hi's Chang-Hon system, Kukkiwon Yudanja Poomsae, and Master Tom Malone's curriculum notes (Tae Kwon Do Personal Notes.docx). Test scope for upcoming rank exams is confirmed directly by your instructor.

1. 1st Dan Black Belt Forms

Kwang-Gae (광개) 39 Movements • Chang-Hon Tul
Ready Stance: Parallel Stance with Heaven Hand (Hanulson) • Diagram: Plus Sign (+)

Named after Gwanggaeto the Great, the 19th King of the Goguryeo Dynasty, who expanded the territory of Korea. The 39 movements refer to the year 391 AD when he ascended the throne. (See dedicated Kwang-Gae Technical Reference).

Po-Eun (포은) 36 Movements • Chang-Hon Tul
Ready Stance: Parallel Ready Stance (Narani Junbi Sogi) • Diagram: Single Line (一)

Named after the pen name of scholar and loyalist Chong Mong-Ju (1337–1392), famous for his unwavering loyalty to the Goryeo Dynasty depicted in his poem. The diagram represents his unswerving loyalty. (Part of the 1st Dan Chang-Hon triad in the private notes P0514; detailed movement sheet available upon instructor confirmation of test requirements).

Ge-Baek (계백) 44 Movements • Chang-Hon Tul
Ready Stance: Parallel Ready Stance • Diagram: Capital I (Line AB and Column CD)

Named after General Ge-Baek, a great general of the Baekje Dynasty. The 44 movements represent his severe and strict military discipline. (Part of the 1st Dan Chang-Hon triad in the private notes P0515; detailed movement sheet available upon instructor confirmation of test requirements).

Peong-Hwa (평화) 66 Movements • UMA Written-Notes Stage: Black Belt
Source: Tae Kwon Do Personal Notes.docx P0464–P0466 • Current Test Stage: Instructor Confirmation

Listed under the Black Belt stage in Master Malone's notes containing 66 movements. Lineage and historical meaning are not independently verified; confirm current test status and execution details with your instructor.

3. 2nd Dan Black Belt Forms

Eui-Am (의암) 45 Movements • Chang-Hon Tul
Ready Stance: Closed Ready Stance D • Diagram: Capital I

Named after Son Byong-Hi, leader of the Korean independence movement on March 1, 1919. The 45 movements refer to his age when he changed the name of Donghak to Cheondogyo in 1905.

Choong-Jang (충장) 52 Movements • Chang-Hon Tul
Ready Stance: Closed Ready Stance A • Diagram: Capital T

Named after General Kim Deok-ryeong (1567–1596) of the Joseon Dynasty. The pattern ends with a left-hand strike symbolizing his tragic death in prison in 1596 after enduring six episodes of severe torture over twenty days following false accusations of treason.

Keumgang (금강) 27 Poom • Kukkiwon Yudanja Poomsae
Ready Stance: Basic Ready Stance (Gibon Junbi-seogi) • Diagram: Chinese Character 山 (Mountain)

Named after Mount Keumgang (Diamond Mountain) and the concept of hardness/indestructibility (Vajra). The movement traces the Chinese character 山, symbolizing diamond-like firmness and majestic stability.

4. 3rd Dan Black Belt Forms

Sam-Il (삼일) 33 Movements • Chang-Hon Tul
Ready Stance: Closed Ready Stance C • Diagram: Roman Numeral I

Denotes the historical March 1st Movement (Samil Movement) in 1919. The 33 movements represent the 33 Korean patriots who organized the Declaration of Independence.

Yoo-Sin (유신) 68 Movements • Chang-Hon Tul
Ready Stance: Warrior Ready Stance B • Diagram: Capital I

Named after General Kim Yoo-Sin, commanding general during the Silla Dynasty who united the Three Kingdoms of Korea. The 68 movements refer to the last two figures of 668 AD, the year Silla unified Korea.

Choi-Yong (최영) 46 Movements • Chang-Hon Tul
Ready Stance: Closed Ready Stance C • Diagram: Plus Sign (+)

Named after General Choi Yong, premier and commander of the armed forces of the 14th century Goryeo Dynasty, renowned for his loyalty, patriotism, and simplicity.

Taebaek (태백) 26 Poom • Kukkiwon Yudanja Poomsae
Ready Stance: Parallel Ready Stance • Diagram: Chinese/Hanja Character 工

Named after Mount Taebaek (historically linked to the sacred Baekdu / Asadal tradition), symbolizing the foundational origin of the Korean people. The floor diagram traces the Chinese/Hanja character 工, symbolizing craftsmanship and the connection between heaven and earth.

5. Academic Bibliography & Reference Literature

Choi, Hong Hi. (1985 / 1999). Encyclopedia of Taekwon-Do (15 Volumes & Condensed Reference). International Taekwon-Do Federation. ISBN: 978-1897307007. Verified source for Chang-Hon Tul geometry, movement counts, and Volume 5 sparring page ranges (pp. 21–223, 278–304).
Choi, Hong Hi. (1965). Taekwon-Do: The Art of Self-Defence. Daeha Publication Company. Primary foundational record for original 20 Chang-Hon patterns.
Kukkiwon (World Taekwondo Headquarters). (2006 / 2022). Taekwondo Textbook & Technical Terminology Glossary. Kukkiwon Research Institute. Official standard for Kukkiwon Poomsae geometry, stance standards, and technical classification.
World Health Organization. (2008). WHO Standard Acupuncture Point Locations in the Western Pacific Region. WHO Regional Office for the Western Pacific. ISBN: 978-9290613831. International nomenclature standard for acupuncture point codes and anatomical locations.
Malone, Tom (Master). Tae Kwon Do Curriculum Guide & Personal Notes (White Belt to 6th Dan Black Belt). Ultimate Martial Arts (USNTF). Controlling written baseline for UMA belt-by-belt one-steps, kicking combinations, grab releases, and breaking standards.
