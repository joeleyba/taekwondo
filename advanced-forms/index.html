2nd Dan & Higher Advanced Black Belt Forms Guide

ITF Chang Hon 24 Patterns & Kukkiwon Dan Poomsae Reference Overview

PO-EUN (포은)
1st Dan Black Belt (ITF)
36 Movements | Diagram: Single Straight Horizontal Line (-)
Historical Meaning: Pseudonym of Jeong Mong-ju (1337-1392 AD), a loyal subject and poet whose poem "I would not serve another master though I were crucified a hundred times" is known to every Korean. The diagram represents his unyielding, single-minded loyalty to his king and country.
GE-BAEK (계백)
1st Dan Black Belt (ITF)
44 Movements | Diagram: Capital I (Modified)
Historical Meaning: Named after General Ge-Baek, a great general in the Paekje Dynasty (660 AD). The diagram represents his strict and severe military discipline in defending his homeland.
EUI-AM (의암)
2nd Dan Black Belt (ITF)
45 Movements | Diagram: Capital I
Historical Meaning: Pseudonym of Son Byong Hi, leader of the Korean independence movement on March 1, 1919. The 45 movements refer to his age when he changed the name of Dong Hak to Chondo Kyo in 1905.
CHOONG-JANG (충장)
2nd Dan Black Belt (ITF)
52 Movements | Diagram: Capital I
Historical Meaning: Posthumous title granted in 1785 to General Kim Deok-ryeong (1567-1596 AD), an Imjin War commander executed in prison at age 29. Pattern ends with a left-hand attack to symbolize his tragic death before reaching maturity.
JUCHE / KO-DANG (주체 / 고당)
2nd Dan Black Belt (ITF)
Juche: 45 Movements | Ko-Dang: 39 Movements
Historical Meaning & Lineage Context: Juche represents the philosophical idea that man is the master of everything. General Choi replaced Ko-Dang (named after patriot Cho Man-sik) with Juche in 1986. Usage is lineage-dependent: ITF-VNA retains Juche, while several ITF-derived bodies retain or restored Ko-Dang.
SAM-IL (삼일)
3rd Dan Black Belt (ITF)
33 Movements | Diagram: Roman Numeral I
Historical Meaning: Denotes the historic date of the March 1st Movement (3/1/1919) which sparked the Korean independence movement. The 33 movements represent the 33 Korean patriots who signed the Declaration of Independence.
YOO-SIN (유신)
3rd Dan Black Belt (ITF)
68 Movements | Diagram: Capital I
Historical Meaning: Named after General Kim Yoo Sin (668 AD), commanding general during the Silla Dynasty. The 68 movements refer to 668 AD when Korea was united. The ready stance signifies a sword drawn on the right side.
CHOI-YONG (최영)
3rd Dan Black Belt (ITF)
46 Movements | Diagram: Capital I
Historical Meaning: Named after General Choi Yong, premier and commander-in-chief during the 14th century Koryo Dynasty, respected for his loyalty and simplicity.
KEUMGANG (금강)
2nd Dan Black Belt (Kukkiwon)
27 Movements | Diagram: Mountain Symbol 山
Historical Meaning: Symbolizes 'hardness' and 'ponderance', derived from Mt. Keumgang and the diamond. Features Crane Stances (*Hakdari-Seogi*) and Diamond Blocks (*Keumgang-Makgi*).
TAEBAEK (태백)
3rd Dan Black Belt (Kukkiwon)
26 Movements | Diagram: Chinese Character 工 (Artisan / Work)
Historical Meaning: Named after Mt. Taebaek, the sacred mountain where Tangun founded Korea. Consists of 26 movements along the 工 diagram line.
