Forms & Poomsae Suite
Form #1 White Belt
CHON-JI (천지)
Pattern Meaning: Chon-Ji literally means 'Heaven and Earth'. In the Orient, it is interpreted as the creation of the world or the beginning of human history, performed by the beginner. Consists of two similar parts (Heaven & Earth).
19 Movements Return: Left Foot
Form #2 Yellow Belt
DAN-GUN (단군)
Pattern Meaning: Named after Dangun Wanggeom, the legendary founder of Gojoseon. Korean tradition dates the kingdom's foundation to 2333 BCE.
21 Movements Return: Left Foot
Form #3 Orange Belt
DO-SAN (도산)
Pattern Meaning: Do-San was the pen name of Korean patriot, educator, and independence activist Ahn Chang-Ho (1878-1938). The 24 movements represent his life and his devotion to Korean education and independence.
24 Movements Return: Right Foot
Form #4 Green Belt
WON-HYO (원효)
Pattern Meaning: Wonhyo was an influential Korean Buddhist monk and scholar who lived from 617 to 686 AD. He helped popularize Buddhism, including Pure Land teachings, among the people of Silla.
28 Movements Return: Right Foot
Form #5 Purple Belt
YUL-GOK (율곡)
Pattern Meaning: Yul-Gok was the pen name of Korean philosopher and scholar Yi I (1536-1584), nicknamed the 'Confucius of Korea'. The 38 movements refer to his birthplace near the 38th parallel, and the diagram represents the scholar (士).
38 Movements Return: Left Foot
Form #6 Blue Belt
JOONG-GUN (중근)
Pattern Meaning: Named after Korean independence activist An Jung-geun (commonly written Ahn Joong-Gun in Taekwon-Do), who assassinated Itō Hirobumi, Japan's first Resident-General of Korea in 1909. An was executed in 1910; the 32 movements represent his age under traditional Korean age counting (30 by modern reckoning).
32 Movements Return: Left Foot
Form #7 Red Belt
TOI-GYE (퇴계)
Pattern Meaning: Toi-Gye was the pen name of Yi Hwang, a prominent Korean Neo-Confucian scholar who lived from 1501 to 1570. The 37 movements refer to his birthplace near the 37th parallel, and the floor pattern represents the Chinese character for scholar (士).
37 Movements Return: Right Foot
Form #8 Brown Belt (2nd Kup)
HWA-RANG (화랑)
Pattern Meaning: Hwa-Rang is named after an elite youth organization of Korea's Silla Kingdom. Members of the Hwa-Rang became influential military and political leaders. The 29 movements refer to the 29th Infantry Division where Taekwon-Do developed into maturity.
29 Movements Return: Right Foot
Form #9 Brown Belt Black Stripe (1st Kup)
CHOONG-MOO (충무)
Pattern Meaning: Choong-Moo was the posthumous title of Admiral Yi Sun-sin of Korea's Joseon Dynasty. He redesigned and deployed the armored turtle ship (Geobukseon) during the Japanese invasions beginning in 1592. The pattern ends with a left-hand attack, traditionally symbolizing his death before he could demonstrate his full potential, constrained by his loyalty to the king.
30 Movements Return: Left Foot
Form #10 1st Dan Black Belt • Chang-Hon Form
KWANG-GAE (광개)
Pattern Meaning: Kwang-Gae is named after Gwanggaeto the Great, the nineteenth king of Goguryeo. Beginning his reign in 391 AD, he expanded Goguryeo to its greatest territorial extent. The pattern has 39 movements, referring to the first two numbers of 391. Its plus-shaped diagram (+) represents the expansion and recovery of territory.
39 Movements Return: Left Foot
Form #11 1st Dan Black Belt (Kukkiwon)
KORYO (고려)
Pattern Meaning: Koryo represents the seonbae: a learned person of upright character who combines scholarly discipline with a strong martial spirit. Kukkiwon connects this lineage through Goguryeo, Balhae, and Goryeo. The floor pattern traces 士, the character for scholar.
30 Movements Return: Left Foot
