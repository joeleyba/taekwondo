The 5 Tenets of Tae Kwon Do

1. Courtesy Ye Ui (예의)

Promoting mutual respect, polite manners, modesty, and treating instructors, seniors, and peers with dignity both inside and outside the dojang.

2. Integrity Yom Chi (염치)

Knowing right from wrong, possessing a clear moral conscience, and maintaining honesty in action and spirit.

3. Perseverance In Nae (인내)

Patience and unyielding commitment to continue striving toward a goal despite difficulties, pain, or fatigue.

4. Self-Control Guk Gi (극기)

Mastering control over one's emotions, actions, and physical techniques in free sparring and daily life.

5. Indomitable Spirit Baekjul Boolgool (백절불굴)

Showing unwavering courage when facing overwhelming odds or injustice, standing up firmly for righteousness.

Student Oath / Creed

  • I shall observe the tenets of Tae Kwon Do.
  • I shall respect the instructor and seniors.
  • I shall never misuse Tae Kwon Do.
  • I shall be a champion of freedom and justice.
  • I shall build a more peaceful world.

Korean Stances Sogi (서기)

Stance Name Korean Name Dimensions & Weight Distribution
Walking Stance Gunnun Sogi 1.5 shoulder lengths long, 1 shoulder width wide. Weight 50% / 50%.
L-Stance Niunja Sogi 1.5 shoulder lengths long. Weight 70% rear leg / 30% front leg.
Sitting Stance Annun Sogi 1.5 shoulder widths wide, parallel feet. Weight 50% / 50%.
Close Stance Moa Sogi Feet touching together side-by-side. Weight 50% / 50%.
Cross Stance Gyocha Sogi Front or rear foot crossed over. Weight heavily on standing foot.
Fixed Stance Gojung Sogi 1.5 shoulder lengths long, equal weight 50% / 50%.
Bending Stance Guburyo Sogi Balancing stance on one bent leg. Weight 100% on standing leg.

Target Heights & Korean Counting

Target Heights (Cha-Ryot / Target)

  • High (Nopunde): Eye level and above (head, neck, temple).
  • Middle (Kaunde): Shoulder level down to navel (chest, solar plexus, ribs).
  • Low (Najunde): Below navel (groin, thighs, shins).
