1. The Five Tenets of Taekwon-Do (Taekwondo Jungshin)

The foundational moral guidelines of Chang-Hon Taekwon-Do (Encyclopedia Vol. 1, p. 11):

1. Courtesy 예의 (Ye-Ui)

Mutual respect, politeness, humility, and maintaining proper etiquette toward instructors and peers.

2. Integrity 염치 (Yeom-Chi)

Knowing right from wrong, honesty, having the conscience to feel guilt when at fault, and standing by truth.

3. Perseverance 인내 (In-Nae)

Patience, continuous effort, enduring hardship, and striving towards a goal without giving up.

4. Self-Control 극기 (Geuk-Gi)

Discipline, emotional mastery, keeping temper in check, and restraint both inside and outside the dojang.

5. Indomitable Spirit 백절불굴 (Baekjeolbulgul)

Courage, standing up against injustice fearlessly, and never yielding in the face of insurmountable odds.

2. Student Oath (Taekwondo Seonseo)

  1. I shall observe the Tenets of Taekwon-Do.
  2. I shall respect the Instructor and Seniors.
  3. I shall never misuse Taekwon-Do.
  4. I shall be a champion of freedom and justice.
  5. I shall build a more peaceful world.

4. Chang-Hon (ITF) Stance Geometry

Standardized stance dimensions from General Choi Hong Hi's Encyclopedia of Taekwon-Do (Vol. 2):

Stance Name Hangul Weight Distribution Foot Positioning & Dimensions Key Characteristics & Biomechanics
Walking Stance (Gunnun Sogi) 걷는서기 50% Front / 50% Rear 1.5 shoulder widths long, 1 shoulder width wide Front foot straight forward, rear foot turned 25° outward; front knee bent over heel.
L-Stance (Niunja Sogi) ㄴ자서기 70% Rear / 30% Front 1.5 shoulder widths long, ~2.5 cm heel-to-heel width Both feet turned 15° inward forming a 90° angle; rear knee bent over toes.
Sitting Stance (Annun Sogi) 앉는서기 50% Left / 50% Right 1.5 shoulder widths wide (inner edges) Both feet parallel pointing straight forward; knees bent outward over toes.
Fixed Stance (Gojung Sogi) 고정서기 50% Front / 50% Rear 1.5 shoulder widths long (1 shoulder width longer base than L-stance) Similar to L-stance with equal weight distribution (50/50) and longer base.
Bending Stance (Guburyo Sogi A) 구부려서기 A 100% Supporting Leg Single leg balance Supporting knee slightly bent; kicking foot tucked to inner knee joint.
Rear Foot Stance (Dwitbal Sogi) 뒷발서기 90% Rear / 10% Front 1 shoulder width long Rear foot turned 15° inward; front foot rests lightly on ball with heel elevated.
Vertical Stance (Soojik Sogi) 수직서기 60% Rear / 40% Front 1 shoulder width long Both legs straight; feet turned 15° inward.
X-Stance (Kyocha Sogi) 교차서기 Weight on stationary, flat supporting foot Crossed legs Supporting foot fully flat; crossing foot touches on ball of foot.

5. Kukkiwon Standard Stance Geometry

Standardized stance dimensions from Kukkiwon Taekwondo Textbook (§2):

Stance Name Hangul Weight Distribution Length & Width Dimensions Technical Execution
Walking Stance (Ap-seogi) 앞서기 50% Front / 50% Rear 1 normal walking step long, 1 fist-width wide Natural walking posture; rear foot angled up to 30° outward.
Forward Stance (Apkubi) 앞굽이 67% Front (2/3) / 33% Rear (1/3) 4.5 foot-lengths long, 1 foot-width (or 2 fist-widths) wide Front knee bent perpendicular to ground; rear leg fully straight with foot angled ~30°.
Back Stance (Dwitkubi) 뒷굽이 67% Rear (2/3) / 33% Front (1/3) 3 foot-lengths long Feet form 90° angle (L-shape); rear knee bent over toes.
Horse-Riding Stance (Juchum-seogi) 주춤서기 50% Left / 50% Right 2 foot-lengths wide between inner edges Both feet parallel straight ahead; knees flexed outward pressing inner thighs.
Tiger Stance (Beom-seogi) 범서기 90% Rear / 10% Front 1 foot-length long Rear foot turned 30° outward; front heel lifted with ball of foot resting lightly.
Crossed Stance (Kkoa-seogi) 꼬아서기 Weight on stationary, flat supporting foot Crossed stance (Dwit-kkoa or Ap-kkoa) One leg crosses behind/in front of the supporting leg.
Crane Stance (Hakdari-seogi) 학다리서기 100% Supporting Leg Single-leg balance Supporting knee bent; opposite foot blade placed against inner knee.
Pushing Ready Posture (Tongmilgi Junbi-seogi) 통밀기준비서기 50% Left / 50% Right Performed in Naranhi-seogi (Parallel Stance) base Slow log-pushing ready posture with open palms at chest height (as in Koryo Poomsae).
